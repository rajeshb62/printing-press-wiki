Plantin-Moretus Family

The Plantin-Moretus family operated the Officina Plantiniana (Plantin Press) in Antwerp from 1555 to 1867 — three centuries of continuous printing under a single family line, spanning twenty-seven generations of printers. Founded by Christophe Plantin (c. 1520-1589), the press passed to the Moretus family (Latinised from Moerentorff) through Plantin’s daughter Martina, who married Jan Moretus. The press produced over 1,500 publications in its first generation alone, and the surviving workshop — the oldest in the world — is preserved as the Plantin-Moretus Museum.^[raw/articles/putnam-books-and-their-makers-vol2.md]^[raw/articles/lorck-handbuch-buchdruckerkunst.md]

Christophe Plantin (c. 1520-1589)

Early Life and Training

Plantin was born in Mont-Louis, near Tours, France, around 1520. He received a good school education that included a working mastery of Latin, the universal literary language of the era. He learned bookbinding under Robert Mace in Caen, Normandy, a town whose university had made it a growing literary and publishing centre. After completing his apprenticeship and undertaking extensive travels, Plantin married Jeanne Riviere around 1546 and moved to Antwerp, where he secured citizenship and became a master-printer, admitted to the Guild of Saint Luke in 1550.^[raw/articles/putnam-books-and-their-makers-vol2.md]^[raw/articles/lorck-handbuch-buchdruckerkunst.md]

Antwerp and the Establishment of the Plantin Press

Antwerp in the mid-sixteenth century was at the height of its prosperity, arguably the leading commercial city of Europe. Before the close of the fifteenth century, thirteen of the sixty-five printers then active in the Netherlands worked in Antwerp; by Plantin’s arrival, an entire quarter of the city was devoted to bookmaking, a circumstance without parallel in Europe.^[raw/articles/putnam-books-and-their-makers-vol2.md]

Plantin began as a bookbinder, soon adding a bookshop. In 1555, he entered upon his career as a printer and publisher with Institution d’une Fille de Noble Maison, followed in the same year by Flores de l’anneo Seneca in Spanish. He purchased a house on the Friday Market (Vrijdagmarkt) and installed a fully equipped printing workshop. His first printer’s mark (1555) showed a tree trunk with a vine winding around it; from 1558 he adopted the compass-and-hand device with the motto Labore et Constantia (By Labour and Constancy), which became one of the most famous printer’s marks in history.^[raw/articles/putnam-books-and-their-makers-vol2.md]^[raw/articles/lorck-handbuch-buchdruckerkunst.md]

The Polyglot Bible (1568-1572)

Plantin’s masterpiece was the Biblia Polyglotta (Royal Bible), an eight-volume edition of the Scriptures in Hebrew, Greek, Latin, Aramaic, and Syriac. It was the most important work ever issued by the printers of the Low Countries and the most scholarly edition of the Scriptures yet put into print. King Philip II of Spain funded the project, promising 21,000 florins (later increased to 24,000 guilders), and appointed his chaplain Benito Arias Montanus as general editor. The typefounder Guillaume Le Be of Paris was commissioned to cut the typefaces.^[raw/articles/putnam-books-and-their-makers-vol2.md]^[raw/articles/lorck-handbuch-buchdruckerkunst.md]

Printing occupied forty workers continuously from 1568 to 1572. The edition comprised 1,213 copies: twelve on imperial vellum, thirty on fine vellum, two hundred on royal vellum from Lyon, and 960 on royal paper from Troyes. Pope Pius V initially refused to sanction publication, blocking the undertaking for years. His successor Gregory XIII proved more favourable, granting approval in 1572. Even then, Professor Leon de Castro of Salamanca denounced Plantin to the Inquisition; Montanus submitted a defence and received clearance only in 1580, narrowly sparing the Bible from the Index.^[raw/articles/putnam-books-and-their-makers-vol2.md]^[raw/articles/lorck-handbuch-buchdruckerkunst.md]

Financially the Polyglot Bible was a failure. Philip II never fully paid the promised subsidy, and five years after publication a considerable proportion of the edition remained unsold. Plantin remarked: “Now that the Bible is complete, I stand with surprise and astonishment before the work, which I would not do again even if someone gave me 12,000 crowns.”^[raw/articles/putnam-books-and-their-makers-vol2.md]^[raw/articles/lorck-handbuch-buchdruckerkunst.md]

Political and Religious Navigation

Plantin operated in a city torn between Catholic Spanish rule and growing Reformed sentiment. In 1562 he was accused of heresy when an edition of Brieve instruction pour prier was printed in his absence. He was condemned in absentio, his goods seized and sold at auction, then acquitted after showing the book had been printed without his knowledge. Friends bought back his equipment, and a new company was organised in 1563 with Plantin as manager.^[raw/articles/putnam-books-and-their-makers-vol2.md]

Plantin came under suspicion repeatedly but always presented evidence of Catholic orthodoxy. Only after his death were documents found showing that as early as 1550 he had been a member of the Family of Charity (La Famille de la Charite), a sect led by Niclaes of Munster, for whom he printed the Spiegel der Gherechtigkeit without his imprint. Throughout his career, he maintained Catholic standing with the theologians of Louvain, Philip II, and the Pope.^[raw/articles/putnam-books-and-their-makers-vol2.md]

In 1570, Philip II appointed Plantin Prototypographer of the Netherlands (proto-typographus), a post that made him the official supervisor of printing. He was to register all masters and apprentices, maintain lists of all works printed (the first and last sheets had to be delivered to him), and set book prices. No remuneration was attached, but the incumbent was freed from billeting soldiers and gained privileged access to approvals. Plantin thereafter styled himself “Printer to the King.” He sought to resign in 1576.^[raw/articles/putnam-books-and-their-makers-vol2.md]^[raw/articles/lorck-handbuch-buchdruckerkunst.md]

The Spanish Fury and Leyden Interlude

In 1576, at the time of the Spanish Fury — the sack of Antwerp by mutinous Spanish troops — Plantin was operating twenty-five presses and employing about 150 workers. The war between Spain and the Dutch rebels devastated Antwerp’s trade. By 1585, when Spanish authority was restored, Plantin had only a single press at work.^[raw/articles/putnam-books-and-their-makers-vol2.md]

In 1583, pressed by creditors, Plantin left for Leyden at the invitation of Justus Lipsius. He purchased the printing establishment where Louis Elzevir had begun work and was appointed Printer to the University of Leyden in 1584. Plantin put Elzevir in charge of his Leyden presses, giving the founder of the Elzevir dynasty invaluable training. In November 1585, he transferred the Leyden office to his son-in-law Franciscus Raphelengius (who succeeded him as Printer to the University and later became Professor of Hebrew) and returned to Antwerp.^[raw/articles/putnam-books-and-their-makers-vol2.md]^[raw/articles/lorck-handbuch-buchdruckerkunst.md]

Typefaces and Typography

Plantin’s types bear comparison with those of Aldus Manutius and Henri Estienne. Lorck describes his italic as “especially elegant, not as cursive as the Aldine”; his roman as “somewhat bold and broad, pleasing to the eye, easily readable, fully the equal of Estienne’s fonts”; his Greek as “finer than Aldus’s.” The precision of casting and spacing was highly praised. He acquired punches and matrices from the best typefounders, including the celebrated Garamond types that became closely associated with his press and influenced type design for centuries.^[raw/articles/lorck-handbuch-buchdruckerkunst.md]

He was the first publisher to associate typography on a large scale with copperplate engraving. The series of copper plates produced under his direction represented an enormous advance in artistic publishing.^[raw/articles/putnam-books-and-their-makers-vol2.md]

Publishing Output

Plantin’s press published over 1,500 works in Latin, Greek, Hebrew, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Dutch, and English. He printed the liturgical works of the Council of Trent, Breviaries, Missals, classical texts (Virgil, Horace, Ovid, Plautus, Juvenal, Terence, Catullus, Tibullus, Propertius), the works of Galen and other medical and scientific authors, the Corpus Juris Civilis in ten volumes, the Thesaurus Theutonicae Linguae dictionary, musical works requiring special fonts, maps by Ortelius and Mercator, and the writings of Church Fathers including Augustine (10 volumes), Jerome (5 volumes), and Tertullian (3 volumes).^[raw/articles/putnam-books-and-their-makers-vol2.md]

His catalogue of 1566 included works in ten languages. The account books record payments to authors and editors — often in copies of the book rather than cash — and reveal the detailed economics of a sixteenth-century publishing house.^[raw/articles/putnam-books-and-their-makers-vol2.md]

Death

Plantin died on 1 July 1589 at age seventy-five. His last work was the first volume of Baronio’s Annales Ecclesiastici. Having no sons, he left the Antwerp business to his son-in-law Jan Moretus, who had managed operations for two years. Raphelengius took the Leyden office; son-in-law Gilles Beys directed the Paris branch.^[raw/articles/putnam-books-and-their-makers-vol2.md]^[raw/articles/lorck-handbuch-buchdruckerkunst.md]

Jan Moretus (1543-1610)

Jan Moretus (Jean Moerentorff) was born in Antwerp on 22 May 1543. He began as a worker in Plantin’s shop and rose through practical competence. Lorck notes that Jan “did not possess deeper knowledge” (besass keine tiefergehenden Kenntnisse) but excelled at maintaining scholarly connections and running the business efficiently. He married Plantin’s daughter Martina.^[raw/articles/lorck-handbuch-buchdruckerkunst.md]

His greatest achievement was securing a ten-year papal monopoly on the printing and distribution of the revised Vulgate north of the Alps, granted by papal breve on 11 March 1597. The 1592 Sixto-Clementine Vulgate had been printed in Rome; Moretus obtained the exclusive right to reprint it for northern Europe, on condition of the greatest care in correction and printing, and that no changes whatsoever be introduced. This monopoly was enormously profitable and sustained the firm through a difficult period.^[raw/articles/lorck-handbuch-buchdruckerkunst.md]

Jan also restored the facade of the Plantin house on the Vrijdagmarkt, preserving the ancient mark of the building while modernising its appearance.^[raw/articles/lorck-handbuch-buchdruckerkunst.md]

Balthasar Moretus (d. 1641)

The son of Jan Moretus, Balthasar was “the learned” (der gelehrte), according to Lorck. Under his direction the press “maintained a highly respected position for a long time” (noch durch lange Zeit eine hochangesehene Stellung).^[raw/articles/lorck-handbuch-buchdruckerkunst.md]

Balthasar cultivated the scholarly and artistic networks that Jan had maintained. He corresponded with Justus Lipsius, the leading humanist of the Low Countries, and commissioned title pages and illustrations from Peter Paul Rubens, who designed some of the most famous title borders and frontispieces of the century. The Rubens-designed title pages for Plantin-Moretus editions are among the high points of baroque book design.^[raw/articles/putnam-books-and-their-makers-vol2.md]^[raw/articles/lorck-handbuch-buchdruckerkunst.md]

A medallion portrait of Balthasar — an eagle holding a star (from the Moretus coat of arms) — still greets visitors entering the Plantin-Moretus Museum vestibule.^[raw/articles/lorck-handbuch-buchdruckerkunst.md]

The Long Decline (1641-1867)

After Balthasar’s death, the firm’s significance faded gradually. Lorck states: “Toward the middle of the 17th century its significance faded more and more, and with the beginning of the 18th century the commercial splendour of the house was extinguished” (Gegen die Mitte des xvii. Jahrhunderts jedoch schwand die Bedeutung mehr und mehr, und mit dem Beginn des xviii. Jahrh. war der geschäftliche Glanz des Hauses erloschen).^[raw/articles/lorck-handbuch-buchdruckerkunst.md]

The presses continued to run, but at greatly reduced volume. The firm remained in the Moretus family, with the 25th generation still active in the early 19th century. External operations continued until 1865-1867, when the city of Antwerp purchased the building and collections for 1,200,000 francs (the national government contributed 200,000, the city 1,000,000).^[raw/articles/lorck-handbuch-buchdruckerkunst.md]

What made the firm’s survival significant, however, was not its commercial vitality in its later centuries but the remarkable preservation of its physical plant. The Moretus family kept everything in place — presses, type, matrices, woodblocks, copperplates, account books, correspondence — even after the business had ceased to be commercially significant. Lorck notes that “externally everything was preserved with great piety in its old state” (Ausserlich wurde jedoch alles mit grosser Pietat in dem alten Stand gelassen). This is why the museum is so complete today.^[raw/articles/lorck-handbuch-buchdruckerkunst.md]

The Plantin-Moretus Museum

The building on the Vrijdagmarkt, purchased by the city of Antwerp in 1867 and opened as a museum in 1877, preserves the oldest surviving printing presses in the world. Lorck provides a detailed description of the house as it stood in the late 19th century:

The courtyard is a large square, three of whose four sides retain their original appearance. Busts of Plantin, Johannes Moretus, Balthasar Moretus, Johann Jakob Moretus, and Justus Lipsius adorn the facades. The composing room still holds type cases filled with type, with composing sticks and galleys in place. Lorck writes: “It is as if work had only been interrupted by the usual midday break and would soon resume” (Es ist, als ware die Arbeit nur von der ublichen Mittagspause unterbrochen und als musste sie baldigst wieder begonnen werden).^[raw/articles/lorck-handbuch-buchdruckerkunst.md]

The museum’s holdings include:

  • The composing room with apparatus for 20-25 compositors, types still in cases
  • Seven wooden presses, two formes still ready for printing
  • The proof-readers’ chamber
  • The type foundry, with casting furnaces still containing remnants of metal
  • Approximately 15,000 woodblocks
  • 7,000 to 8,000 copperplates
  • Portraits by Rubens, van Dyck, and others
  • The business archives from 1566 to 1865, including journals, ledgers, binders’ books, catalogue drafts, and correspondence drafts

The archives are of exceptional value: they include the journal and account books from three centuries, correspondence with scholars and authors across Europe, and — remarkable for the history of printing — correspondence between the Moretus family and the Elzevir family of Leiden regarding the purchase of type. Lorck notes that a typographical curiosity in the library is a volume compiled by Jan Moretus in 1576, dedicated to Plantin, containing a collection of the title pages of every book printed by Plantin up to that year — an early specimen book for printers.^[raw/articles/lorck-handbuch-buchdruckerkunst.md]

Significance

The Plantin-Moretus firm is unique in the history of printing: no other press operated continuously for three centuries under a single family line, and no other press preserved its physical plant, equipment, and archives so completely. The firm’s business records from 1566 to 1865 are the most complete set of early printing house accounts in existence, and they have enabled scholars to reconstruct the detailed economics of early modern book production.

The firm’s typographic legacy is equally significant. Plantin’s types — especially the Garamond types he acquired and popularised — influenced European type design for centuries. The Plantin typeface, revived in the twentieth century for machine composition, is named after him. His pioneering use of copperplate illustration on a commercial scale set new standards for illustrated books. And the survival of his house as a museum provides an incomparable record of how a sixteenth-century printing shop actually worked.
